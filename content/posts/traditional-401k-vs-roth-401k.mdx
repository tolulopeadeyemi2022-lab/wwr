The traditional 401(k) vs Roth 401(k) decision comes down to one core question: do you want your tax break now or later?

## The Core Difference: Tax Timing
Every dollar in a 401(k) eventually gets taxed. The only variable is when you pay the bill.
* **Traditional 401(k):** Contributions are made with pre-tax dollars, lowering your current taxable income. Withdrawals in retirement are taxed as ordinary income.
* **Roth 401(k):** Contributions are made with post-tax dollars from your paycheck. Withdrawals in retirement are completely tax-free if qualified.

## Side-by-Side Comparison
| Feature | Traditional 401(k) | Roth 401(k) |
|---|---|---|
| When taxed | In retirement, on withdrawals | Now, on contributions |
| Today's paycheck | Slightly larger (taxes deferred) | Slightly smaller (taxes paid now) |
| Retirement withdrawals | Taxed as ordinary income | Tax-free (if qualified) |
| Income limits | None | None |

## Which One Should You Choose?
* **Choose Traditional If:** You are in a high tax bracket right now, expect your income to drop in retirement, or want a bigger paycheck today.
* **Choose Roth If:** You are early in your career in a lower bracket, expect future tax rates to rise, or want tax-free retirement income.
* **Choose Both If:** You want tax diversification and flexibility by splitting contributions between both account types.

## 2026 Contribution Limits
For 2026, the employee contribution limit for both traditional and Roth 401(k)s is **$24,500** combined. Workers aged 50 and older can add an $8,000 catch-up contribution.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I contribute to both a traditional and a Roth 401(k)?
Yes. Many plans allow you to split your contributions between both accounts as long as your combined total stays within the annual limit.

### Does my employer match go into the traditional or Roth account?
Employer matching contributions are generally deposited into a traditional account, growing tax-deferred until withdrawal.

## Key Takeaways
Traditional accounts reduce your taxes today while Roth accounts give you tax-free income tomorrow. Choosing the right one depends on your current tax bracket versus your expected bracket in retirement.

An S&P 500 index fund is a single investment that owns a small piece of 500 of the largest companies in America, designed to match the market's overall performance rather than trying to beat it.

## What the S&P 500 Actually Is
The S&P 500 is a stock market index that tracks 500 of the largest publicly traded companies in the United States across nearly every industry, weighted by their size.

## How an S&P 500 Index Fund Works
* **Pooled Money:** Your money combines with thousands of other investors to purchase shares in all 500 companies in matching proportions.
* **Instant Diversification:** One purchase gives you immediate exposure to major tech, healthcare, financial, and retail leaders.
* **Low Costs:** Because it tracks an index passively rather than paying active managers to pick stocks, expense ratios remain remarkably low (often under 0.05%).

## Historical Performance
Historically, the S&P 500 has returned an average of around 10% annually before inflation (and closer to 7% after inflation) over long, multi-decade periods.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is an S&P 500 index fund good for beginners?
Yes. It offers instant diversification, historically strong long-term returns, and low fees without requiring you to research individual stocks.

### Can I lose money in an S&P 500 index fund?
Yes. Because it tracks the broader stock market, it can lose value during market downturns, though it has historically recovered over extended time horizons.

## Key Takeaways
An S&P 500 index fund simplifies investing by giving you broad market exposure through a single, low-cost asset that harnesses long-term economic growth.

## What To Do Next
Open a brokerage or retirement account, compare a few low-cost S&P 500 options based on their expense ratios, and set up your first contribution.

Late payments, high credit utilization, and collections accounts do more damage to your credit score than almost everything else combined. 

## What Hurts Your Credit Score the Most, Ranked
* **Bankruptcy:** Drops scores by 130 to 240 points and lingers for 7 to 10 years.
* **Late Payment (30+ Days):** Costs 60 to 110 points, staying on your report for 7 years.
* **Collections Account:** Lowers scores by 50 to 150 points.
* **High Utilization:** Costs 20 to 70 points, though it fades within 1 to 2 billing cycles once resolved.
* **Closing an Old Card / Hard Inquiries:** Shorter-term impacts ranging from 5 to 40 points.

## How to Fix Each Type of Damage
* **Fixing Late Payments:** Pay immediately to stop further reporting thresholds, and ask for a goodwill adjustment if it's your first offense.
* **Fixing High Utilization:** Pay down balances above 30% or request a credit limit increase to lower your overall utilization ratio instantly.
* **Fixing Collections:** Request written debt validation and negotiate a "pay for delete" agreement before sending payment.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What hurts your credit score the most, a late payment or high utilization?
A late payment causes more severe and long-lasting damage because payment history carries the heaviest weight in scoring models.

### How long does a single late payment affect my score?
It stays on your report for seven years, but its negative impact fades significantly after the first one to two years of consistent, on-time payments.

## Key Takeaways
Credit damage follows a predictable hierarchy. While late payments and collections linger, high utilization can be fixed rapidly by managing your balances.

## What To Do Next
Check your credit utilization across all open cards today, and pay down any balance sitting above 30% of its limit before your next statement closes.

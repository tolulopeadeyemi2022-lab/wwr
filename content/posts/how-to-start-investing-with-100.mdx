A hundred dollars doesn't feel like much, but it's more than enough to start investing. Waiting for a "better" starting amount usually costs more than starting small ever would.

## Why $100 Is Enough to Start
What matters more than the size of your first deposit is the number of years your money stays invested. Because of compound growth, your money earns returns, and those returns start earning returns of their own over time.

## Step 1: Get Your Financial Foundation in Order First
Before your $100 goes near an investment account:
* Clear high-interest debt (such as credit card balances above 20% interest), as paying that off is a guaranteed return.
* Build a small cash cushion ($500 to $1,000) so emergencies don't force you to sell your investments at a loss.

## Step 2: Choose the Right Account for Your Goal
1. **Retirement:** A Roth IRA offers tax-free growth and tax-free withdrawals in retirement.
2. **Employer Match:** Prioritize a 401(k) employer match immediately if available—free money beats any other account.
3. **Short-Term Goals:** Use a taxable brokerage account if you might need the money within the next five years.

## Step 3: Pick an Investment That Matches $100
* **Low-Cost Index Fund:** Holds a small slice of hundreds or thousands of companies at once, offering instant diversification.
* **Fractional Share of an ETF:** Lets you buy a partial share of a broader market fund with your $100.
* **Watch Expense Ratios:** Choose funds with low annual management fees (ideally under 0.10%) to keep more of your returns.

## Step 4: Automate What Comes Next
Set up a recurring monthly transfer of $100 into your account. Automating the process removes the temptation to skip a month and builds lasting financial momentum.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is $100 really enough to start investing?
Yes. Most major brokerages have no minimum deposit requirement, and fractional shares let you buy partial ownership of funds.

### What's the safest way to invest $100 as a complete beginner?
A low-cost S&P 500 index fund or total market ETF is widely considered one of the most beginner-friendly options for broad diversification.

## Key Takeaways
Starting with $100 is enough to build a lifelong habit. Choose the right account, pick a diversified index fund or ETF, and automate your contributions to let compound growth work in your favor.

## What To Do Next
Open a brokerage account this week, set up your first $100 deposit into a low-cost index fund, and schedule an automatic monthly transfer.

You pay every bill on time and have never missed a payment in your life, yet your score still isn't where you expected it to be. There's a good chance the culprit is sitting quietly on your statement in a number most people never think to check.

## What Is Credit Utilization, Really?
Credit utilization is simply the relationship between two numbers: how much credit you have available, and how much of it you are currently using. It makes up 30 percent of your FICO score, second only to payment history's 35 percent. 

* **Credit Utilization Ratio:** Total credit card balances divided by total credit limits, expressed as a percentage.
* **The Ideal Target:** Keeping utilization under 30 percent works for a good score, while single digits are ideal for an excellent score.

## Overall Utilization vs. Per-Card Utilization
Scoring models evaluate both your overall utilization across all cards combined and your utilization on each individual account. A single maxed-out card can drag your score down even if your combined utilization looks reasonable overall.

## What Counts as "Good" Utilization?
* **0%:** Neutral to slightly negative on some models, as a small amount of activity shows responsible use.
* **1% to 10%:** Ideal range associated with the strongest scores.
* **11% to 29%:** Good, generally not a major drag on your score.
* **30% to 49%:** Starts to noticeably lower your score.
* **50% to 74%:** Significant negative impact.
* **75% and above:** Severe negative impact, treated similarly to a maxed-out account.

## How to Lower Your Credit Utilization Ratio Fast
* **Pay before your statement closes:** Issuers typically report balances on the statement closing date rather than the due date. Making an extra payment early keeps your reported balance low.
* **Ask for a credit limit increase:** A higher limit with the same spending automatically lowers your ratio.
* **Spread balances across cards:** Moving a portion of debt from a near-maxed card to one with plenty of room prevents individual accounts from crossing steep penalty thresholds.
* **Keep old cards open:** Closing unused accounts shrinks your total available credit and spikes your utilization.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does credit utilization reset every month?
Yes. Utilization is a monthly snapshot rather than a running history, meaning it can change dramatically from one billing cycle to the next.

### Does paying in full every month count as utilization?
It can. If your statement closes with a balance before you pay it in full, that balance is what gets reported to the bureaus.

## Key Takeaways
Credit utilization is the second most important factor in your credit score. Monitor both your overall ratio and per-card percentages, pay down balances before your statement closing date, and keep old accounts open to protect your available credit.

## What To Do Next
Calculate your total credit utilization ratio today. If any card sits above 30 percent, make a payment before your next statement closes to see an immediate impact on your score.

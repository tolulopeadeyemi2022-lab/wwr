A 529 plan is a state-sponsored investment account designed specifically for education savings, offering tax-deferred growth and tax-free withdrawals for qualified expenses.

## What Is a 529 Plan?
* **Tax-Advantaged Growth:** Contributions grow tax-free, and withdrawals used for qualified education expenses avoid federal income tax entirely.
* **Parental Control:** As the account owner, you retain full control over the funds, unlike custodial accounts that transfer ownership to the child at adulthood.

## The Two Types of 529 Plans
* **Education Savings Plans:** Flexible investment portfolios that can be used at eligible schools nationwide and abroad.
* **Prepaid Tuition Plans:** Allow families to lock in today's tuition rates at specific in-state public colleges.

## What Counts as a Qualified Expense?
* **College & K-12:** Covers tuition, mandatory fees, room and board, books, computers, and up to $20,000 per year for K-12 tuition.
* **Student Loans:** Can be used to repay up to $10,000 in student loans over a beneficiary's lifetime.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I lose money in a 529 plan?
Yes. Since 529 funds are invested in market portfolios, balances can fluctuate, though age-based portfolios automatically shift toward conservative assets as college approaches.

### What happens if my child doesn't go to college?
You can change the beneficiary to another family member, hold the funds for future use, or roll over up to $35,000 lifetime into a Roth IRA for the beneficiary.

## Key Takeaways
A 529 plan is a powerful tool for tackling climbing education costs through tax-deferred growth and flexible withdrawal options.

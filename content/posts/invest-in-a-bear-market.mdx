Your portfolio just dropped 20 percent, and your phone won't stop buzzing with negative news. Learning how to invest in a bear market is not about predicting when stocks will bottom out, but about building a plan sturdy enough that you don't need to predict anything at all. 

## Why Bear Markets Feel So Worse Than They Are
A bear market is usually defined as a drop of 20 percent or more from recent highs, occurring roughly once every four to five years. While losses hurt roughly twice as much as equivalent gains feel good, every bear market in modern U.S. market history has eventually been followed by a recovery to new highs.

## Practical Steps to Invest During a Bear Market

### 1. Separate Emotions From Strategy
Give yourself a rule of no portfolio changes within 24 hours of feeling panicked. Selling during a downturn permanently locks in your losses, while staying invested only risks a loss on paper.

### 2. Check Your Asset Allocation
A bear market is a good excuse to verify if your mix of stocks, bonds, and cash still matches your actual risk tolerance and timeline. A common rule of thumb is to subtract your age from 110 to estimate a reasonable stock percentage.

### 3. Keep Contributing on Autopilot
Continuing to buy on schedule allows you to practice dollar-cost averaging, automatically buying more shares when prices are lower. This removes the temptation to pause contributions out of fear.

### 4. Resist the Urge to Sell
Selling during a bear market converts a temporary decline into a permanent loss and requires you to correctly guess when to get back in. Missing just the 10 best market days over a 20-year period can cut your total returns roughly in half.

### 5. Consider Buying More Gradually
If you have extra cash sitting around and will not need it for at least five years, consider spreading additional purchases over several months to buy assets at a discount.

### 6. Rebalance Instead of Reacting
Rebalancing means selling a bit of what has grown relatively larger and buying more of what is now underweighted to bring your allocation back to your original target. 

### 7. Protect Your Cash Reserve First
Confirm your emergency fund has three to six months of essential expenses sitting in a high-yield savings account. This prevents you from having to sell investments at a loss to cover an unexpected expense.

### 8. Turn Off the Noise
Financial news leans toward the dramatic because fear gets clicks. Checking your portfolio balance once a month or quarter gives you enough information without the anxiety of daily volatility.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Should I stop investing during a bear market?
Generally, no. Continuing regular contributions means you're buying shares at lower prices, which can meaningfully boost long-term returns once the market recovers.

### Is it a good idea to move everything to cash during a downturn?
Moving to cash locks in your losses and requires correctly timing your return to the market, which almost no investor does consistently well.

## Key Takeaways
Bear markets are uncomfortable, but panic selling turns a temporary decline into a permanent loss. Continuing your regular contributions, keeping your emergency fund intact, and checking your portfolio less often are the best ways to protect your wealth.

## What To Do Next
Start by confirming your emergency fund covers at least three months of expenses, checking that your automatic contributions are still running, or simply closing your news app for the rest of the day.

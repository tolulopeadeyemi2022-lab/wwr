You open a brokerage app for the first time, ready to finally start investing, and within thirty seconds you're staring at words like "expense ratio," "asset allocation," and "capital gains" as if they're written in another language. 

## Why These Terms Matter
You don't need an economics degree to invest well. But not knowing basic vocabulary makes every decision feel riskier than it actually is. Once these words stop sounding foreign, investing starts feeling like something you're capable of doing.

## Essential Investing Vocabulary
* **Stock:** A small piece of ownership in a company.
* **Bond:** A loan you make to a company or government that pays back with interest over time.
* **Index Fund:** An investment holding a broad basket of stocks or bonds designed to match a market index like the S&P 500.
* **ETF (Exchange-Traded Fund):** Similar to an index fund, but it trades throughout the day on an exchange like an individual stock.
* **Diversification:** Spreading your money across different investments so one bad pick doesn't sink your portfolio.
* **Compound Interest:** Your money earning money, then earning money on those earnings too.
* **Expense Ratio:** The annual fee a fund charges, expressed as a percentage of your investment.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need to understand every investing term before I start?
No. Start with the basics, like what a brokerage account is and how index funds work, then build your vocabulary naturally as questions come up.

### How much money do I need to start investing?
Many brokerages now allow you to start with small amounts, thanks to fractional shares and zero account minimums.

## Key Takeaways
Investing terms aren't designed to confuse you. Stocks, bonds, index funds, and compound interest form the core building blocks you need to know to get started with confidence.

## What To Do Next
Pick one term you've been avoiding, look up how it applies to an account you already have, and take your first step toward building your portfolio.

You've spent years raising kids or paying off debt, and somewhere along the way, investing kept getting pushed to next year. Now you're in your 40s, and it feels like the window already closed. It hasn't. 

## Why Starting in Your 40s Isn't a Setback
While someone starting at 25 has a mathematical advantage, your 40s come with real advantages of their own. Your income is typically higher, your financial priorities are clearer, and you have roughly 20 to 25 working years left before retirement—plenty of time to build significant wealth.

## Step 1: Get Your Foundation in Order First
Before investing a single dollar:
* Build an emergency fund covering three to six months of essential expenses.
* Get high-interest debt (generally anything above 7% or 8%) under control.
* **The Exception:** If your employer offers a 401(k) match, prioritize capturing that free money even before your emergency fund is fully built.

## Step 2: Prioritize Your Accounts in the Right Order
1. **Your 401(k) Match:** Contribute enough to capture the full employer match immediately.
2. **A Roth IRA:** Lets your investments grow completely tax-free, with withdrawals in retirement coming out tax-free.
3. **Max Out Your 401(k) Further:** Increase your contributions once your match and IRA are handled.
4. **Taxable Brokerage Account:** Provides unlimited additional room to invest without contribution caps.

## Step 3: Figure Out How Much to Invest
A common benchmark suggests having two to three times your annual salary saved by age 40, and six to seven times by age 50. If you are behind, focus on a realistic monthly contribution that closes the gap over your remaining working years. Consistency matters more than a past head start.

## Step 4: Choose Investments That Match Your Timeline
With 20 to 25 years until retirement, a portfolio overly weighted toward cash or bonds risks losing purchasing power to inflation. Low-cost index funds tracking broad market indexes provide the diversification and growth needed over a multi-decade timeline.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is it really possible to retire comfortably if I start in my 40s?
Yes. With consistent contributions, employer matches, and catch-up contributions after age 50, building a solid retirement fund remains completely realistic.

### Should I pay off my mortgage before investing?
If your mortgage interest rate is relatively low (historically under 6%), most guidance favors investing additional money rather than aggressively paying down low-interest debt.

## Key Takeaways
Starting to invest in your 40s gives you a 20 to 25-year runway that is more than enough time to build wealth through consistent contributions. Prioritize employer matches, max out tax-advantaged accounts, and avoid the trap of being too conservative out of fear.

## What To Do Next
Log into your retirement account or open a Roth IRA this week, set a sustainable contribution percentage, and let consistency do the work.
